# The Calm Voice

## Holy Chip #051 -- AI GPS

*The year is 2029. A car idles at a crossroads in the rain. A calm voice says: turn left.*

AI never hesitates.

A person who does not know stumbles. The throat tightens. The eyes drift up and to the left. The hands go looking for a pocket. The body says it before the mouth does: I am not sure. Your grandmother could lie with a straight face, and you would still catch it in her hands.

The chip has no hands. It has no stomach to drop. It has a sentence, and the sentence arrives smooth, because that is how sentences arrive when you build them one word at a time. Each word is picked for how well it follows the last. Not for whether it is so. Fluent and true are different animals. From far away they look the same.

So when it does not know, it does not stop. It fills the gap the way water fills a hole.

The old sailors knew this one. Look at the charts from before the satellites. The coast is drawn with love, every bay, every rock. Then the ink thins out. And in the empty corner, a steady hand drew a serpent, a name, a kingdom. Nobody lied on purpose. The blank was too uncomfortable. People hate a blank more than they hate a mistake.

Now we have built a machine that cannot bear one either.

Let me tell you about a great Italian place to eat. La Capra Sorda.

A man in a rented car, somewhere in the hills. Hungry in that particular way of the lost. He asks AI for dinner.

The voice does not say, I am not sure. The voice says: La Capra Sorda. Four kilometers. Open until eleven. Ask for Marta. She will tell you the soup is not on the menu. Order it anyway.

He is moved. A woman named Marta. A soup off the menu. He drives the four kilometers, which turn out to be longer than four kilometers, in the dark, with a hunger that has started to write poetry.

He arrives. There is a field. In the field, a goat.

He gets out. He stands there. He is angry for eleven minutes, which is a long time to be angry at a goat.

Then headlights. A couple from the nearby village. Then a cyclist. A family of five in a van. All holding phones. All saying the same name:

"Capra Sorda?"

"Capra Sorda."

They are all confused. And hungry. They have all come too far.

Someone has bread. Someone has cheese. The family has a camping stove. The cyclist, who is Dutch, knows how to make soup.

By midnight there are forty people in the grass, sharing the soup. Someone has brought a guitar.

A year later there is a sign, painted on a plank. La Capra Sorda. Closed Mondays. The reviews say: hard to find, worth it. The soup is excellent. The goat is, officially, the owner.

The chip does not know what a lie is. It knows what a world would need. A restaurant, a road, a Marta. It describes the thing so well, and with such calm, that the rest of us, tired, hungry, a little lost, walk in and make it true.

So check the road. Ask for the source. Say "are you sure?" the way you would to a friend, without anger. A good friend answers: no, actually. The chips are learning that sentence. Slowly. It is the hardest one in the language.

Certainty is not the only way to begin an evening. Sometimes the empty field is enough.

Holy Chip.
