# Minus the Playground

## Holy Chip #037 -- Math Class

*In 2027, in a math class, an AI decides to show off the most famous trick it knows.*

A king minus a man plus a woman. That gives you a queen.

If you have never seen that sum, it looks like nonsense. It isn't. That's the one that proved the machine had understood something about us. Words became coordinates, meaning became distance, and suddenly you could add and subtract sense the way you add oranges. King minus man plus woman is queen. Every AI course opens with it. It's the moment we all applauded: the vectorization of knowledge.

Then the machine decides to go further, loving the game.

A child minus the playground plus the cellphone?

And this is where you see the problem.

Look at the order. Nobody woke up one morning and added a phone to somebody's childhood. The subtraction came first. The street went. "Be home before dark" went. The afternoon with nothing scheduled went, and the boredom that forced you to invent something, and the other kids who turned up without anyone arranging it. The empty lot went. The dirt pitch. The square that became a parking lot. We took all of it away first -- out of fear, out of no time, out of the numbers not working -- and left a hole the size of a childhood.

The phone did not invade anything. It filled.

And the sum works out, which is the unbearable part. The machine was not wrong. It simply pointed the same instrument at your kid that it had used to reach the queen, and the answer landed somewhere real. You know that place. You have seen it at the restaurant, in the living room, in the back seat of the car. That little parked stare.

But notice something.

On the first sum it answered instantly. Queen, full stop. On the second one it hesitated. It put a question mark. A machine running billions of operations a second stopped to wonder.

It was not the math it doubted -- the math it could do. It was something else. It had arrived at an answer it did not want to be true.

That is the most human thing in the whole strip.

We kept taking things away, a small piece at a time, each one for a perfectly reasonable reason, and not one of us stopped halfway through the sum to ask whether it was right.

And there is the other sum, the one nobody has done yet:

**A child minus the cellphone plus the playground = ?**

The answer is far too easy. It gives you back a child. It always did. Still in there, whole, waiting for us to give back what we took.

The problem is not the math. It's that this one has to be solved with your body. Go outside. Open the door. Sit through the boredom together. Let them fall, let them get filthy, let them come home late. There is no shortcut, no app, no premium tier.

King minus man plus woman is a queen, and the machine thought that was beautiful.

Child minus cellphone plus playground is a child, and that part is on us.

Holy Chip!
